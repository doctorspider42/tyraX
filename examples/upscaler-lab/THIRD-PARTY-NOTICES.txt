  res/models/depot/Textures/truck.png         (128x128)

      Derivative works ("kit-bashes") assembled from the kit's own modules -
      permitted without condition by CC0, and recorded here so the result is
      reproducible rather than mysterious. The textures are Kenney's own files,
      copied unchanged.

      block-west.obj is built from the kit's `wall-a` family and block-east.obj
      from its `wall-b` family - each module a 1x1x1 cell, placed on a grid at
      one module per world 1.4 m:

        block-west   wall-a, wall-a-window, wall-a-open, wall-a-roof
        block-east   wall-b, wall-b-window, wall-b-roof

      Three mesh operations were applied and nothing else. Kenney's OBJ export
      writes every face TWICE, so the duplicate was dropped. A face with another
      module on the far side of it was dropped as well - it is inside the mass
      and can never be seen. And each window module was rotated so its front
      looks at open air. The vertices, UVs and material assignments that remain
      are the modules' own.

      trucks.obj is three whole kit models placed at a transform and merged
      into one file - `truck-grey` twice and `truck-grey-cargo` once - so the
      parked lot costs the PS2 one draw call instead of three.

      Every merge groups faces by material, which is what keeps each building to
      one bag submit per texture on the console.


Universal Base Characters (NOT used - recorded for the next person)
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Quaternius' Universal Base Characters [Standard] is CC0 1.0 and was evaluated
for this example's animated model. Its free tier ships a rigged mesh - a skin
and a 130-bone skeleton - but ZERO animation clips (the glTF has no `animations`
array; the FBX has no AnimationStack and no AnimationCurve). The rigged, ANIMATED
sources are in the paid SOURCE version. A rig with no clips renders as a statue,
so it was not used. This is recorded so nobody re-derives it.
